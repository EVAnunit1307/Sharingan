# Articulated people and sensor fusion

Implementation checkpoints, 2026-09-20. See `person-pose-fusion-plan.md` for the original proposal. Hardware accuracy and frame-rate targets remain acceptance goals until measured with people in the room.

## Data flow

The Pi keeps YOLOX person detection and LD2450 acquisition. `/pose/frame` returns an unannotated JPEG, person boxes, source session, generation, frame ID and capture time from the **same inference result**. The laptop runs MediaPipe Pose Landmarker Full, binds landmarks to those boxes, and attaches accepted results to persistent fused tracks. The Quest receives additive `spatial_people.tracks_version = 1` data alongside the legacy arrays.

Camera and radar each remain sufficient to create a contact. Radar-only figures are explicitly unverified or previously seen, with estimated posture. Camera absence inside the field of view is reported; it does not prove an unobstructed view or veto radar. The dashboard shows rejection reasons, uncertainty ellipses, velocity, source ages and the exact pose-frame overlay.

Radar uses a constant-velocity Kalman filter, global one-to-one assignment, innovation and jump gates, and three-of-five sample confirmation. Stationary contacts remain eligible. Fused IDs survive short source transitions. Predictions support association; they do not renew visibility. Position deadlines remain 500 ms for radar and 750 ms for camera. Camera joints expire after 350 ms independently of fresh root observations.

## Human rendering

The existing CC0 Blender Studio base mesh is rigged with anatomical bones and at most four weights per vertex. Camera landmarks supply directions, with fixed bone lengths, elbow/knee bend limits and smoothed transforms. Missing limbs blend toward a procedural idle/walk estimate driven by filtered velocity. The fallback is not a measured gait or radar-derived skeleton. Facing from a usable camera pose is distinct from travel direction.

The material, identity colours, through-wall visibility, bounding boxes and corner labels remain shared with the existing presentation. Manual/static figures remain supported. The `CAMERA POSE` / `ESTIMATED POSE` label identifies the available evidence; body height stays assumed until supported by configured floor geometry.

The configured GPU skin path failed the isolated body-only render check on desktop. CPU skinning is used as a bounded workaround, with a minimum LOD of 1 (3,711 vertices per person; LOD2 has 2,042). All LOD CPU buffers are retained for cooking and LOD streaming is disabled. Eight bodies therefore skin at most about 30,000 vertices. Both camera and inferred poses pass paired-eye body-only captures; Quest multiview and frame time still require wearer validation. Extreme poses can distort this automatically weighted mesh; it is not a personalized body reconstruction.

## Moving rig

The left controller is mounted rigidly with its **aim ray**, rather than its grip axis, parallel to camera/radar forward. Select `rig_motion_mode: left_controller` on the laptop. The Quest requests a session-only alignment at startup:

1. Hold the rig upright (camera up is room up; forward must not point nearly vertically).
2. Use the **right controller** to put the white probe on the radar centre. The probe sits 12 cm ahead along the right controller's aim ray, so the controller itself need not touch or overlap the hardware. The **left controller stays mounted on the drone**.
3. Press the right index trigger, then hold the rig and probe still for 0.65 seconds. The app averages the local mount offset and rotation over at least 16 samples. Rig movement above 2 cm or 3 degrees, probe-offset movement above 1.5 cm, mount-rotation changes above 2 degrees, or a tracking/sample gap over 100 ms restart the sampling window. The HUD shows progress. These thresholds stabilize capture; they are not an accuracy guarantee.
4. Review the white origin and mint forward arrow following the rig. Move the rig gently to check that the white centre stays on the radar and the arrow points along the camera/radar. Press the **right index trigger again to accept**, or A to start over. Capturing alone does not activate people placement.
5. After acceptance, trigger shows the origin guide for six seconds; A recalibrates after remounting; B changes visibility. Hidden removes the guide and clears any unfinished capture window. A completed review can survive a brief tracking gap, but accepting or rendering it requires fresh tracked poses. Recenter and suspension clear calibration.

The left aim pose supplies forward, gravity supplies initial upright roll, and the probe supplies the positional offset. The full rigid transform follows subsequent translation, yaw, pitch and roll. The HUD and minimap remain **headset-centered**, with no fixed room-origin calibration, map-centering toggle, or change to the XR tracking origin. The guide uses the existing two-sided, through-wall world material in both eyes. Physical marking and controller-to-sensor parallel alignment still limit accuracy; the capture window only reduces sample jitter. The camera remains 32 mm right, 12.8 mm forward and 14 mm lower than the radar in the fusion configuration.

The app requires fully tracked left-controller position, not inertial-only rotation. Loss of tracking hides figures immediately, drops pose history and waits for fresh source samples after recovery. Recenter, suspension, mode/reference changes or application restart require alignment again. A bounded two-second history associates readings with their reported capture age; it never extrapolates or bridges tracking gaps over 100 ms. Repeated sample IDs keep their original world placement. World-space motion supplies fallback gait and heading; sensor-relative motion from moving the rig does not directly animate walking. The dashboard displays the Quest's alignment/tracking report with a 750 ms expiry.

This remains a **same-floor estimate**. Radar measures two-dimensional returns, not elevation; transformed horizontal positions are placed on the Quest session floor and labelled `EST. FLOOR`. Camera-only box range remains monocular. Floor-ray ranging is disabled for a moving rig because camera height/pitch are not synchronized to capture on the laptop. Reported source ages omit unmeasured downstream network latency, so fast rig motion can still produce error. No flight-control or motor code is used. Replays without recorded controller poses cannot be positioned using the current live controller.

A conservative background optical-flow alarm can invalidate a stationary registration. Person boxes are masked. The alarm is **not** six-degree-of-freedom localization and cannot prove that a quiet image means the rig is stationary. It latches until explicit registration reset. `untracked` mode keeps relative diagnostics but pauses Quest placement. `stationary` mode retains the two-floor-point anchor workflow. Camera height, pitch and intrinsics improve floor projection only for a valid stationary configuration; unknown height disables it.

## Reproduce

Controller input diagnosis (2026-09-20): the physical left controller moved in Quest Home, but the app reported either a hand-interaction profile or no active profile, without usable controller poses. UE 5.7's `UEDeployAndroid` adds the optional hand-tracking feature and permission independently of Meta's `ControllersOnly` setting. `WallhackControllers_APL.xml` removes both declarations. Removing hand support alone did not resolve the reported failure. The HUD now uses persistent input contexts declared in `DefaultInput.ini`, so OpenXR can bind them before session creation instead of relying on runtime-created mappings. Common plus exactly one of Sensor, Navigation or Demo is activated; the other modes are registered but inactive. `Build/create_controller_inputs.py` generates the 22 assets using UE 5.7's `DefaultKeyMappings` structure. The APK verifier checks both the packaged startup configuration and cooked contexts/actions.

The startup-binding build established valid right-controller tracking with application focus, but the mounted left controller still had no active held-controller profile. The wearer then held the mounted left grip, rested their thumb on it and pressed X: the app changed from `LEFT CONTROLLER POSITION UNAVAILABLE` to the alignment instructions. This isolates the held/unheld transition as the next issue; startup bindings and controller-only manifest declarations alone are insufficient.

`WallhackXR` now loads at `PostConfigInit`, before OpenXR initialization. In `-WallhackSensorPeople` mode it requests `XR_META_detached_controllers` and `XR_META_simultaneous_hands_and_controllers`. One physical aim action binds both `/user/hand/left` and `/user/detached_controller_meta/left`, only for Touch, Touch Pro and Touch Plus controller profiles. The action set participates in UE's normal attachment and synchronization, preserving the existing button bindings. Simultaneous tracking is resumed at session begin, after Meta's plugin has initialized it in a paused state. Separate action spaces explicitly select the held and detached subactions; these are created after UE attaches the input bindings. Each uses the same physical aim origin and calibration. This avoids relying on runtime association of an unspecified subaction, which returned no located pose during a confirmed held-controller test on this Quest.

The mounted pose requires a focused running session, an active action, and valid **and tracked** position and orientation from `xrLocateSpace`. It is located in the active HMD tracking space and transformed to world once. It never uses hand joints or saved last-good poses. Existing tracking-loss/history/recenter behavior remains in effect. If the extension is unavailable, the strictly validated held-controller pose remains available. The right calibration probe uses the active XR runtime's fully tracked right aim pose, with a 12 cm forward offset.

`Wallhack.ControllerStatus` logs active profiles, position validity, tracking status and VR focus on demand; mounted diagnostics additionally report extension support, resume success, held/detached subaction activity, the explicitly selected source, location flags and the locate call's result. Development builds log classification/quality changes as they happen, allowing a delayed release failure to be retrieved afterward. It does not add HUD clutter. The controller-only Android manifest remains deliberate: supporting an unheld physical controller does not bind hand interactions to the mount. A wearer-confirmed 30-second table-rest test passed with fully tracked detached poses; longer-duration behavior and physical sensor alignment remain unverified.

API references: [Khronos detached-controller behavior](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/OpenXR-Docs/blob/main/specification/sources/chapters/extensions/meta/meta_detached_controllers.adoc) and [Meta simultaneous tracking](https://developers.meta.com/horizon/documentation/native/android/native-multimodal/).

Use Python 3.12 in an isolated environment and install `GroundStation/requirements-pose.txt`. Download verified task bundles with:

```powershell
python Build/download_pose_models.py
python -m GroundStation.server --pi-http http://172.20.10.3:8766 --pi-websocket ws://172.20.10.3:8765/ --pose-model Saved/PersonPose/models/pose_landmarker_full.task
```

`--record <new.jsonl>` records source packets and pose results with local receipt timing. Add `--record-pose-frames` to include exact JPEGs for overlay replay. Recordings are local and are not made unless requested through the flag. Replay with `--replay <file>`; original source ages and joint expiry still apply. Calibration should match the recording. Video replay needs OpenCV; model inference is not rerun during result replay.

`Build/prepare_articulated_human.py` runs in Blender; `Build/import_articulated_human.py` runs in Unreal's Python commandlet. The skeleton is saved explicitly along with the mesh. Asset provenance remains in `SourceAssets/HumanSilhouette/BUNDLE_README.txt`. Model downloads use Google's official version-1 MediaPipe task bundles with pinned SHA-256 checksums in `Build/download_pose_models.py`.
